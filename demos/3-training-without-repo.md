Congrats on the hire. The first two weeks decide most of what kind of setter this person becomes, so it's worth planning them out. Here's a simple structure you can adapt.

## Before Monday

- Set up their logins: CRM, dialer, calendar, Slack or team chat, and any scheduling links.
- Write down (or record) your current script, your ideal customer, and your top 5 to 10 objections with the answers you want used.
- Pull 10 to 20 call recordings: a mix of great calls, average calls, and lost calls.
- Decide their targets for week 1, week 2, and the end of month 1, so nothing is a surprise.

## Week 1: Learn, listen, then start

**Day 1: Context**
- Walk them through the business: what you sell, who buys it, what it costs, and why people buy.
- Explain exactly where the setter fits. What counts as a qualified appointment? What happens after they book it?
- Tour the tools. Have them make a test booking end to end.

**Day 2: The script and the offer**
- Go through the script line by line and explain *why* each part is there.
- Cover qualification criteria in detail. Who should they book, and who should they politely disqualify?
- Homework: listen to 5 recorded calls and note what the setter did well and badly.

**Day 3: Shadowing**
- They sit in on live calls with you or your best setter or closer.
- Debrief after each call: what happened, what they'd do differently.

**Day 4: Role play**
- Run role plays for an hour or more. Start easy, then add the common objections.
- Have them practice the opener until it sounds natural, not read.
- Record the role plays so they can hear themselves.

**Day 5: First live calls**
- Let them take a small batch of live conversations, ideally warmer leads first.
- You or a senior team member listen in, or review the recordings the same day.
- End the week with a 30-minute review: wins, gaps, and one or two focus points for next week.

## Week 2: Volume with feedback

- **Ramp up activity.** Move toward full daily volume (dials, conversations, or messages, depending on your setup). Something like 50% of target on Monday, building to 80 to 100% by Friday.
- **Daily huddle (15 min).** Review yesterday's numbers and listen to one or two calls together.
- **Daily role play (20 to 30 min).** Focus on whatever objection or stage they struggled with the day before.
- **Track the basics:** conversations, appointments booked, show rate, and how many booked calls the closer says were actually qualified.
- **Closer feedback loop.** Have your closer tell the setter which appointments were good and which were a waste of time. This is the fastest way to tighten their qualifying.
- **End of week 2 review.** Compare their numbers to the targets you set. Agree on what "good" looks like for weeks 3 and 4.

## A few things that make a big difference

- **Feedback beats training.** Short, frequent call reviews do more than long training sessions.
- **Fix one thing at a time.** Give them one or two focus points per day, not ten.
- **Protect their confidence early.** New setters get rejected a lot. Point out what they did right, not only what went wrong.
- **Watch quality, not just bookings.** A setter who books lots of unqualified calls is costing your closer time.
- **Write it all down.** Whatever you explain this week becomes your onboarding doc for the next hire.

## Simple 2-week checklist

| Item | Done by |
|---|---|
| Tools set up and tested | Day 1 |
| Script and qualification criteria understood | Day 2 |
| 10+ calls listened to | Day 3 |
| Role plays on top objections | Day 4 |
| First live calls taken | Day 5 |
| At or near full daily volume | Day 10 |
| Week 2 numbers reviewed against targets | Day 10 |
