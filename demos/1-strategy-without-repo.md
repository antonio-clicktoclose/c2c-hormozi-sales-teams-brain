Handing off sales is one of the biggest steps you'll take as an owner. Most handoffs fail because the owner hires a person before the sales process exists on paper. Start with the process, then hire.

## 1. Write down how you sell today

You close deals now, so your process is the starting template. Before you hire anyone, capture:

- **Who buys.** The ideal customer, their main problem, and the usual objections.
- **Where leads come from.** Referrals, ads, inbound calls, outbound, events.
- **Each step of the sale.** First contact, discovery call, proposal or demo, follow-up, close.
- **What you say.** Record your next 5 to 10 sales calls. These recordings become the training material and the base for a script.
- **Your numbers.** Leads per month, close rate, average deal size, and how long a deal takes.

If you can't describe the process, a new hire can't follow it.

## 2. Decide what you're handing off

"Sales" covers several jobs. Pick the part that frees the most of your time:

- **Setting appointments.** Someone qualifies leads and books calls, and you still close. This is often the easiest first step.
- **Closing.** Someone runs the full sales call.
- **Account management.** Someone handles repeat customers and upsells.

Many owners hand off appointment setting first. They hand off closing after the system proves it works.

## 3. Get your tools in place

- A **CRM** (customer relationship management system) to track every lead and deal. HubSpot, Pipedrive, GoHighLevel and Close are common choices.
- **Call recording**, so you can review calls and coach.
- A simple **pipeline**, with clear stages that match your process.

## 4. Set up pay and targets

- Use a base salary plus commission, or commission only for some roles. Commission only attracts fewer strong candidates unless lead flow is strong.
- Set clear targets: calls per day, meetings booked, close rate, and revenue.
- Make sure the commission still leaves you a healthy margin.

## 5. Hire the right person

- Look for proof that they sold something similar: a similar price point, buyer type, or sales cycle.
- Run a role-play in the interview. Have them sell your offer, or something simple, to you.
- Check references and ask about real numbers they hit.
- Think about hiring two people, not one. One hire can fail for reasons that have nothing to do with your process. Two hires show you whether the problem is the person or the system.

## 6. Train and ramp up slowly

- **Week 1 to 2:** They listen to your recorded calls, learn the offer, and sit in on your live calls.
- **Week 3 to 4:** They run calls while you listen and give feedback after each one.
- **Month 2 onward:** They run calls alone, and you review recordings each week.

Expect a ramp period of 60 to 90 days before they hit full targets.

## 7. Manage with numbers

- Hold a short weekly meeting to review the pipeline and the activity numbers.
- Review a few call recordings each week.
- Compare their close rate with your own baseline from step 1.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Hiring a "rainmaker" and expecting them to figure everything out.
- Handing off with no CRM or tracking.
- Stepping away fully in the first month.
- Not having enough leads to keep a salesperson busy. A salesperson can't succeed without lead flow.
