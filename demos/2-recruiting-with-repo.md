## Short answer

Hire one **closer** first: an in-home sales rep who runs the appointments you run now. Pay them half base and half commission, with a flat amount per sale. Before they start, write down the script you already use in homes. That script is your first fix.

All timestamps link to the free YouTube version of Hormozi's training. His numbers are his team's numbers, not benchmarks for HVAC.

## First, your numbers don't add up

Here is the math with what you gave me:

- 80 appointments a month x 35% close rate = 28 sales a month
- 28 sales x $11,000 = $308,000 a month
- $308,000 x 12 = **about $3.7M a year**

You said you do about $2.4M. That's $1.3M less, even before any service or repair revenue.

$2.4M / 12 / $11,000 = about 18 system sales a month. On 80 appointments, that's a close rate of about **23%**, not 35%.

Possible causes: some of the 80 visits are repairs or quotes that aren't full replacements, the average ticket is lower, or the summer peak makes the monthly numbers uneven. Pull last year's real count of sits and sold jobs. The pay math below uses your stated 35%. If your real rate is closer to 23%, each rep closes fewer jobs and the per-sale rate should go up.

## Who to hire

**A closer, not a setter.** Hormozi's rule: if you have lots of inflow and you close, hire closers first. If you have little inflow and must create demand, hire setters first. [1:22:01](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=4921) You have 120 warm inbound leads a month from Google and referrals. Your bottleneck is your own time in homes, so you need someone who can sit and sell.

**Use the "three Ps plus one" scorecard.** [1:16:31](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=4591) Has the person sold to:

- **This prospect:** homeowners, ideally in Phoenix. If you can only get one P, pick this one.
- **This process:** in-home consults on warm leads. Warm traffic like referrals is easier than cold. [1:17:30](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=4650)
- **This product:** HVAC, or a close cousin such as solar, roofing, windows or water treatment.

You want two of the three. Plus one: at least one year of sales experience. [1:18:02](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=4682)

**Then check three traits.** [1:18:27](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=4707)

- **Intelligence:** how fast they use feedback. Coach them live in the interview and see if they change.
- **Work ethic:** did they practice your script, and do they follow up fast?
- **Humility:** a candidate who wants to rewrite your script before they have sold with it is a bad sign.

Hormozi's newer view is to hire the person who needs the fewest resources to reach your standard. [1:20:06](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=4806)

**One or two?** If cash flow allows, he hires more than one at once. Training five takes as long as training one, and you can see who is good. If all of them fail, the process is the problem. [1:15:50](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=4550) For you, two reps would split your 80 sits. One rep plus you is the safer start if cash is tight. You decide that from your bank balance, not from the training.

**You've already proven the process.** His rule is "never two news": don't put a new person on a new process, because if it fails you can't tell which one broke. [1:15:33](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=4533) You're closing in homes today, so the process is proven. It only stays proven if the new rep runs the same process you do. That's why the script comes first.

## How to pay them

The training gives no pay level for HVAC or for Phoenix. You set the on-target earnings (OTE) from what that skill costs in your market. OTE means the total a rep earns in a year when they hit target.

**His structure:**

1. **Split OTE 50/50.** Half base, half variable. The base covers their bills, so they don't push homeowners out of need. [1:31:44](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=5504)
2. **Pay on closed sales,** not on appointments run. [1:29:55](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=5395)
3. **Use a flat amount per sale, worked out from the math.** [1:32:30](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=5550)
4. **If you must pay a percentage,** base it on cash collected up front (or up-front gross margin), not on contract value. [1:35:30](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=5730) For HVAC, that means a job that's canceled or never paid pays no commission.
5. **Add accelerators later,** only once you track good numbers across a team. [1:35:00](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=5700)

**The math with your numbers.** I used $100,000 OTE only because it's the number in his example. Swap in your own.

- $100,000 OTE = $50,000 base + $50,000 variable
- $50,000 / about 50 working weeks = $1,000 a week in commission at target

| Rep runs | Sits per week | Closes per week at 35% | Per-sale rate | As % of $11,000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 40 sits a month (half yours) | 9.2 | 3.2 | about $310 | 2.8% |
| 80 sits a month (all yours) | 18.5 | 6.5 | about $155 | 1.4% |

Inputs: sits per month x 12 / 52 = sits per week. Sits per week x 35% = closes per week. $1,000 / closes per week = per-sale rate.

If your true close rate is 23%, the half-load rep closes about 2.1 a week and the rate becomes about $480 per sale.

He also argues it's cheaper to pay better. You pay more per rep, but you get better applicants, faster ramp, higher close rates and less churn. [1:28:39](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=5319)

Check Arizona wage and commission rules with an employment advisor before you sign anything. This isn't legal or tax advice.

## What to have ready before they start

### 1. A written script (your first fix)

"No script, no sales team." Hormozi's gyms only scaled once someone wrote the script down and taught it. [0:10:51](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=651) A script is the definition of good. Without it, a rep is aiming at a target they can't see. [0:16:03](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=963)

Write down what you already say in homes, in four parts:

- **Intro:** who you are, why you're here, the agenda, and a small yes. [0:29:02](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=1742)
- **Discovery:** the most important part. Find the gap between the homeowner's current state and the state they want: high bills, a unit that keeps failing, rooms that won't cool. If there's no urgency, the rep didn't dig deep enough. [0:32:02](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=1922)
- **Pitch:** about two minutes, not longer. [0:35:50](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=2150)
- **Ask:** state the price, ask, then stop talking. [0:37:55](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=2275)

Keep it short. His team usually cuts 50 to 75% of the scripts they review, and sales go up. [0:30:01](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=1801)

**Why this is the one first fix:** you're the only person who knows how you close at 35% (or 23%). If that stays in your head, the new rep makes up their own version, and you can't tell whether a slow month is the rep or the pitch.

### 2. A "frame" the homeowner sees before and during the visit

A frame is what the prospect watches before the conversation. His version is a video sales letter (VSL). [0:17:30](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=1050)

- He uses home services as his example: show a VSL on an iPad while you walk to the truck, then close. Don't email quotes. [0:27:01](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=1621)
- Put a short video after booking too, so the homeowner is home and ready. [0:25:13](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=1513)
- The simplest version is about 10 minutes of before and after examples. [0:24:55](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=1495) For you, that's past installs, old units against new ones, and real customers' bills.
- Add an FAQ that answers your common objections, like "I'll get two more quotes" or "Can I just repair it?" [0:22:01](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=1321)

### 3. The pay plan, in writing

Use the table above with your real OTE. Put it in the job post and the offer.

### 4. An onboarding plan

His team runs a 14-day bootcamp before new reps touch live leads. He says the same process works for closers. [1:55:38](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=6938)

- Read the script out loud 25 times a day. [2:00:03](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=7203)
- Role play with them three times a day. You play the homeowner. [1:58:00](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=7080)
- Teach the prospect more than the product: Phoenix homeowners' worries and the words they use. [1:57:01](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=7021)
- A pass or fail script test at the end of week one. [1:59:02](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=7142)
- Week two: shadowing live calls. [2:05:02](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=7502) *Adapted for in-home sales:* they ride along on your appointments, then you ride along on theirs.

**Don't hand them all the leads.** Gates protect your leads while the rep learns. [2:05:29](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=7529) His closer gates start with lower-quality leads and move up to the best inbound leads. [2:19:02](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=8342) *Adapted for you:* start them on a capped number of Google leads. Keep the referrals, your warmest leads, until their close rate holds.

**Say it on day one:** this is a performance seat with 60 days to prove it. [2:10:30](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=7830) The best early red flag is a rep who tries to "improve" the script on day one. [2:11:01](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=7861)

### 5. A simple hiring process

- **Job post as a sales letter,** not HR copy: vision, who it's for, pay, the real hours. [1:37:20](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=5840)
- **A required 60-second selfie video.** [1:37:54](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=5874)
- **A skills call:** send the script first, role play, and interrupt with feedback many times. Unprepared or late means the call is over. [1:44:26](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=6266)
- **A culture call** where you "unsell" the job and watch how they take one candid piece of feedback. [1:46:03](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=6363)
- **An offer with pre-work,** so you see who does homework before day one. [1:47:49](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=6469)

**Where to find them:** his first choice is employee referrals, with the bonus paid at a point like day 90, not at hire. [1:51:02](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=6662) Your techs and installers may know good in-home reps. For a one-off hire, he says a recruiter on contingency (never on retainer) is a good fit. [1:52:02](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=6722) [1:54:10](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=6850)

**How much to spend to recruit:** his formula is one year of gross profit per rep x the percent of hires who make it, divided by 3. [1:49:52](https://youtu.be/oVPXgRs0IdI?t=6592) I can't run it because I don't know your gross margin per job. Plug in your number.

## What to do this week, in order

1. **Fix your numbers.** Pull last year's sits, sold replacements and replacement revenue. Find your real close rate (35% or about 23%).
2. **Write your script.** Record your next three in-home appointments (check Arizona consent rules first), then write the intro, discovery, pitch and ask from what you actually say. Cut it hard.
3. **List your top five objections** and your answer to each. That becomes your FAQ.
4. **Set the pay plan.** Choose an OTE for your market, split it 50/50, and work out the per-sale rate from the table with your real close rate.
5. **Write the job post** with the pay, the hours and the 60-day performance seat. Ask your techs for referrals and call one contingency recruiter.
