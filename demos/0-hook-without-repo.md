Hiring your first salesperson is a big step. The most common mistake is hiring too early, or hiring someone and expecting them to "figure out sales" for you. Here's a practical way to approach it.

## 1. Make sure you're ready

Before you hire, check these:

- **You've sold it yourself.** Ideally you (the founder or owner) have closed a meaningful number of deals. You know who buys, why they buy, what objections come up, and how long it takes.
- **There's a repeatable process.** Even a rough one: where leads come from, what the first call looks like, how you follow up, how you close.
- **You have enough leads.** A salesperson with nothing to work on will fail, no matter how good they are. Know whether they'll get leads from you (inbound, referrals, marketing) or need to generate their own.
- **You can afford the ramp.** Most reps take 3 to 6 months to become fully productive. Budget for that runway.

## 2. Decide what role you actually need

"Salesperson" can mean very different jobs:

- **Closer / account executive:** Takes qualified leads and closes them. Best if you already have lead flow.
- **SDR / appointment setter:** Does outreach and books meetings for someone else to close.
- **Full-cycle rep:** Finds, qualifies and closes their own deals. Harder to find, and usually more expensive.
- **Account manager:** Grows and keeps existing customers.

For a first hire, many small businesses do best with a full-cycle rep or a closer, depending on how many leads they already have.

## 3. Look for the right profile

For a first hire, prioritize:

- **Experience selling something similar:** a similar price point, buyer type and sales cycle. Selling $500 software to small businesses is very different from selling $200k contracts to enterprises.
- **Comfort with ambiguity.** Big-company reps often rely on structure, tools and marketing support you don't have yet. A "builder" type who has worked at a startup or small company usually fits better.
- **Coachability and curiosity.** They'll need to learn your product and give you feedback on what's working.
- **Track record you can verify.** Ask for specific numbers: quota, attainment, deal sizes, and how they hit them.

## 4. Set up pay that makes sense

A common structure is base salary plus commission, often with a target split around 50/50 or 60/40 (base/variable) for closing roles.

- Make sure their target earnings are competitive for your market.
- Keep the commission plan simple enough to explain in one sentence.
- Consider a reduced quota or a guaranteed draw for the first few months while they ramp.
- Avoid commission-only offers unless that's normal in your industry. They tend to attract weaker candidates and lead to high turnover.

## 5. Run a solid hiring process

- **Write a clear job description:** what they'll sell, to whom, how leads come in, and what success looks like at 90 days and 12 months.
- **Source widely:** your network, LinkedIn, referrals from customers or peers, and job boards. Great salespeople often aren't actively looking, so reach out directly.
- **Interview for real skills:** ask them to walk you through a recent deal from first contact to close.
- **Do a role-play or mock call.** Give them basic info about your product and have them run a discovery call or pitch. You'll learn more in 20 minutes than in an hour of questions.
- **Check references,** especially former managers, and ask about their actual numbers.

## 6. Set them up to succeed

Many first hires fail because of a weak onboarding, not a bad hire.

- **Document what you know:** your pitch, common objections, ideal customer profile, pricing and case studies.
- **Let them shadow you** on calls, then have them run calls while you watch.
- **Give them a CRM** from day one (HubSpot, Pipedrive or similar) so activity and pipeline are visible.
- **Set clear expectations:** activity targets early on (calls, meetings), then shift to results (pipeline, closed revenue).
- **Meet weekly** to review the pipeline and coach on specific deals.

## 7. Know how to judge it

Give it a fair window, usually 90 days for early signs and 6 months for real results. Look at:

- Are they doing the activity?
- Is the pipeline growing?
- Are deals moving forward?
- Are they learning and improving?

If activity is high but results are low, the problem may be your process, leads or offer. If activity is low, it's more likely a fit issue.

## A few common mistakes to avoid

- Hiring before you've proven you can sell it yourself
- Hiring a "big name" rep who's never worked without a big support system
- Giving no leads and expecting instant results
- Making the comp plan too complex
- Hands-off management in the first few months
