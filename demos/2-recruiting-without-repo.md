## First, a quick check on your numbers

Here is what your figures imply:

- 80 appointments x 35% close = about 28 sales a month
- 28 x $11,000 = about $308,000 a month, or about $3.7M a year

You said you do about $2.4M a year. So one of these numbers is probably off. Your close rate may be lower, your average ticket may be lower once you count repairs and smaller jobs, or not every "sale" turns into a full install. Pull the real numbers from your CRM or invoices before you set a salesperson's targets and pay. You want the plan built on facts, not on a good month.

The bigger point stands either way. **About 40 leads a month never get a visit.** If even half of those got seen and closed at a normal rate, that's roughly 7 more jobs a month. At $11,000 each, that's around $75,000 a month in missed revenue. A good salesperson pays for themselves fast.

## Who to hire

Hire an experienced **residential HVAC comfort advisor** (some shops say "in-home sales" or "replacement specialist").

Look for:

- **2+ years selling residential HVAC replacements in the home.** Not commercial, not a counter or phone job. In-home, one-call close selling is its own skill.
- **A track record they can prove.** Ask for their close rate, average ticket and monthly revenue. Good reps know these numbers cold. Ask for pay stubs or W-2s if you want to confirm.
- **Comfort with financing.** In Phoenix, a lot of replacements close on financing. They should be at ease presenting payment options.
- **Good-better-best presenting.** They should show options, not one price.
- **Honesty and follow-up habits.** Your reputation is on the line in every home they walk into.

Who **not** to hire first:

- A technician you "promote" to sales, unless they already sell well on service calls. Some techs are great at it. Many hate it.
- A general sales rep with no HVAC background. They'll take months to learn the product, and you don't have months.
- A friend or family member because they're available.

Where to find them: competitors' reps (Phoenix has a lot of big replacement shops), HVAC job boards, Indeed, LinkedIn, and your equipment distributor. Distributor reps often know who the strong salespeople are and who is unhappy where they work.

Interview tip: have them run a mock sales call with you as the homeowner. You'll learn more in 20 minutes than in an hour of questions.

## How to pay them

The standard in residential HVAC is **commission-heavy, with a base or draw during ramp-up.**

A common structure:

- **Commission: about 8% to 10% of the sale price** for leads you provide. Some shops pay on gross profit instead (often 15% to 25% of the job's gross profit). Paying on gross profit protects your margins, because the rep can't just drop the price to close.
- **Base or draw while they ramp:** something like $2,000 to $4,000 a month for the first 60 to 90 days. A **draw** is an advance against future commission. A **base** is guaranteed pay on top. A draw is cheaper for you. A base is more attractive to strong candidates.
- **Bonus for self-generated leads:** a higher rate, such as 12%, for jobs they source themselves.
- **Spiffs (small bonuses)** for add-ons like maintenance plans, air quality products or duct work.

A good comfort advisor in a market like Phoenix can earn **$100,000 to $200,000+ a year.** Don't fear that. If they're earning $150K, they're probably selling $1.5M to $2M for you.

Rules to set in writing:

- Commission is paid when the job is **installed and paid**, not when the contract is signed.
- Clear rules on cancellations, chargebacks and financing fees.
- Who sets the price floor, and whether they can discount. Limit discounts or require your approval.
- What happens to commission on jobs in progress if they leave.

Have an employment lawyer or HR service review the plan. Commission and draw rules have legal requirements, and Arizona has its own wage laws.

## What to have ready before they start

1. **A written pricebook.** Every system, size and option with a set price. If prices live in your head, a new rep can't sell. Use good-better-best tiers.
2. **A defined sales process.** Write down how you run a call: arrival, inspection, load calculation, presentation, price, financing, close and follow-up. Even a one-page outline helps. Better yet, let them ride along on 5 to 10 of your calls first.
3. **A CRM with every lead tracked.** They need to see the appointment, the customer's info and the history. Field service software like ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro or Jobber works. You also need it so you can measure their close rate.
4. **A lead assignment rule.** Decide who gets which leads. Many owners keep referrals or repeat customers at first and give the new rep the Google leads. Be fair and clear, or you'll create resentment.
5. **Financing set up.** Have at least one financing partner with options for different credit levels, and train the rep on it.
6. **Sales tools.** Tablet, proposal software, brochures, warranty info and photos of your past installs.
7. **A truck or vehicle allowance**, branded if possible, plus gas or mileage rules.
8. **Install capacity.** If they add 7 to 10 jobs a month, can your crews handle it? Hiring a closer with no one to install is a fast way to get bad reviews.
9. **Targets and a review rhythm.** Set clear goals for the first 30, 60 and 90 days. Close rate, average ticket and revenue. Meet weekly at first.
10. **A trial period.** Make clear in the offer that the first 90 days is a ramp and review period.

## A simple rollout plan

- **Weeks 1 to 2:** They ride along on your calls and learn the pricebook, products and software.
- **Weeks 3 to 4:** They run calls while you ride along and coach after each one.
- **Month 2:** They run calls solo. You review each lost deal with them.
- **Month 3:** They carry a full load. You judge them against the targets.

## One more thing

Hiring this person changes your job. You go from "the guy who sells" to "the guy who builds the sales team." Give yourself time each week to coach, check the numbers and fix the process. If this first hire works, you'll likely want a second one within a year, and the systems you build now make that much easier.
