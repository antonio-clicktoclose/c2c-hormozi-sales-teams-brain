# 05. Onboarding, ramp gates, cutting, career path and promotion

Source: Section 3 "Sales Training" [M2 39:56 · YT 1:55:03] to [M3 24:58 · YT 2:20:37]. His team onboards setters (SDRs); he says the same process works for closers.

## The 14-day bootcamp (stranger to SDR)

- **Why a bootcamp.** New reps mixed into the team got random tips and got overwhelmed. Isolate them for 14 days of full-day training. [M3 0:00 · YT 1:55:38] [M3 0:25 · YT 1:56:02]
- **What they do:** game tape, script memorization, role play, handbook, and prospect training. [M3 1:17 · YT 1:56:57]
- **Teach the prospect more than the product.** Their avatars, pains and words. The pitch is only about two minutes, so most selling is about the prospect. Use their lingo. [M3 1:21 · YT 1:57:01] [M3 2:21 · YT 1:58:00]
- **Manager role plays with them three times a day.** [M3 2:21 · YT 1:58:00] [M3 7:08 · YT 2:02:47]
- **Days 0 to 7** (his plan): [M3 2:40 · YT 1:58:17] [M3 2:54 · YT 1:58:30] [M3 3:24 · YT 1:59:02]
  - Day 0: team reaches out, weekend one-on-ones, homework.
  - Day 1: sell them the process (values, path, ramp, gates).
  - Day 2: intro and early friction points.
  - Day 3: discovery, the heaviest day. "The pain is the pitch."
  - Day 4: bridge, pitch, booking (book a meeting from a meeting).
  - Day 5: full script under pressure.
  - Day 6: first real dials.
  - Day 7: script graduation, pass or fail: script adherence, tonality, recaps, top five objections.
- **Read the script out loud 25 times a day** (about two hours). "Boredom means you're doing it right." One SDR did 50 reps a day and learned the script faster than anyone. [M3 4:23 · YT 2:00:03] [M3 4:49 · YT 2:00:28]
- **Days 8 to 14:** more time on the phones, shadowing live calls, game tape review. [M3 9:23 · YT 2:05:02]
- **Day 8 survey** by private message: start, stop, continue. A pattern (more than three reps) gets fixed in the playbook for the next cohort. A single rep's issue is handled one-on-one. [M3 8:13 · YT 2:03:51] [M3 8:21 · YT 2:04:00]

## How to role play

- Frame (what, how, why), model (manager does it), copy, practice until right, recap. "There's only two ways to train, modeling and role play." [M2 29:58 · YT 1:45:03] [M3 5:22 · YT 2:01:00]
- During bootcamp, the prospect is easy ("NPC"), with scripted answers, until the whole script is automatic. Then speed rounds: each rep does the next part, no pauses. [M3 6:11 · YT 2:01:50] [M3 6:22 · YT 2:02:02]
- Blackout method for memorizing: black out one more word each read. [M3 6:43 · YT 2:02:22]

## Ramp gates (do not give new reps all the leads)

- A new rep once burned through leads with 50 connects a day, and no one showed up. Gates protect leads. [M3 9:54 · YT 2:05:29] [M3 10:23 · YT 2:06:00]
- **Gate 1:** cap at 30 connects a day, which should give 10 true conversations (3+ minutes) and 2 to 3 sets. Hold it for a week (about 10 sets). [M3 10:33 · YT 2:06:11]
- **Gate 2:** cap at 3 sets a day until show rate clears 60%. Target 70 to 75%. [M3 10:58 · YT 2:06:35]
- **Fully ramped (his targets):** 12+ sets a week, 70%+ show rate, 3+ closes a week, within two months. Shout out every gate cleared. [M3 11:21 · YT 2:07:00]
- **Dial cadence:** they cut from 14 dials to 7 per lead. The last seven dials produced only about 10% of sales, so the cut doubled team capacity. Their cadence: 3 double dials day 1, 2 on day 2, 1 on day 3, 1 on day 4, plus SMS and email. An email reply triggers an immediate call. [M3 11:50 · YT 2:07:28] [M3 12:00 · YT 2:07:39] [M3 12:26 · YT 2:08:03]

## When to cut

- "All elite teams are built through elimination, not addition." You get the team you tolerate. [M3 13:02 · YT 2:08:42] [M3 13:41 · YT 2:09:19]
- Make it quantitative. One week behind during ramp = a two-week coaching plan with clear targets. Bad culture fit or cannot take feedback = cut now. [M3 14:14 · YT 2:09:54]
- Feedback must name observable behavior ("you talk too fast and don't raise your voice on questions"), not labels. [M3 14:24 · YT 2:10:01]
- State on day one that this is a performance seat with 60 days to prove it. Extend only for a rare, very coachable, hard-working rep. [M3 14:50 · YT 2:10:30] [M3 15:06 · YT 2:10:46]
- Best early red flag: on day one, the rep tries to improve the script. [M3 15:22 · YT 2:11:01]

## Career path and perks

- For any repeated role: junior and senior versions of each title, each with a pay bump. Many small milestones beat two big ones. He would rather have 12. [M3 16:05 · YT 2:11:43] [M3 16:44 · YT 2:12:21]
- Perks: for sales, better leads. The best leads go to the best reps. Lead tiers rotate monthly at the manager's discretion. [M3 17:14 · YT 2:12:54] [M3 18:22 · YT 2:14:01]
- A high ceiling, even for a few people, attracts exceptional people. [M3 18:01 · YT 2:13:40]
- Paths: individual contributor, sales leader, process or strategy roles. [M3 18:49 · YT 2:14:28] [M3 19:19 · YT 2:15:00]

## Promotion (setter to closer)

- Qualifying makes you eligible, not promoted. You must be "undeniable": all KPI minimums held for a full quarter, 100% admin compliance, no repeat process violations, living the values. [M3 19:52 · YT 2:15:30] [M3 20:26 · YT 2:16:02] [M3 20:40 · YT 2:16:19]
- Next step: one self-set a day, then closing some of their own sets. They must do both jobs at once. Then a 7-day closer bootcamp led by the closer manager, inside a 30-day cycle. [M3 20:59 · YT 2:16:38] [M3 22:38 · YT 2:18:16] [M3 22:57 · YT 2:18:32]
- **Closer ramp gates:** 2 to 3 self-set closes a week for three weeks, then the lowest-quality outbound leads, then medium and high outbound, then inbound. His bar: 60% show rate, 90% "off rate" (as transcribed), 30%+ close rate. [M3 23:25 · YT 2:19:02] [M3 23:40 · YT 2:19:18]
