# 01. Frame and script (what you need before you hire)

Source: Hormozi, "The Most Valuable Sales Training You'll Ever Watch", Section 1 and Section 2a. Timestamps are H:MM:SS in the YouTube video `oVPXgRs0IdI`.

## Big picture

- **Work backwards from the big version.** He shows how a $100M+ sales org runs. Take the parts that fit your size now and fill gaps over time. Build the foundation for the building you want later. [M1 7:58 · YT 0:08:01] [M1 9:01 · YT 0:09:01]
- **Advanced means never skipping the basics.** Founder-led sales convert well because the founder does all the basics. The goal is to get every rep to do the same basics. [M1 9:55 · YT 0:09:57]
- **The system has not changed in years.** Human buyers and how people learn do not change. Only the technology changes. [M1 8:47 · YT 0:08:50]

## You need two things before recruiting: a frame and a script

- **No script, no sales team.** He tried to let others "just do what I do" in his gyms. It did not work until a partner wrote the script down and taught it. Then other people could close. [M1 10:47 · YT 0:10:51] [M1 11:59 · YT 0:12:01]
- **Common owner objections and his answers:** [M1 12:29 · YT 0:12:30] [M1 12:58 · YT 0:13:00]
  - "They will sound scripted." Only if they do not know it. If they know the words, they add active listening (repeat the last thing said, with an upward tone).
  - "Every situation is different." Every closed sale follows the same path: agenda, their problem, your solution, ask. Either you write the script or the rep makes one up. [M1 13:32 · YT 0:13:35]
  - "No one can sell like me." They cannot sell like you, but they can sell as well as you.
  - "My reps are not experts." Use a process frame, not an expert frame. The rep is "the secretary protecting the surgeon", or "the mechanic who has to look under the hood first". [M1 14:29 · YT 0:14:31] [M1 15:08 · YT 0:15:12]
  - "We have a long sales cycle." Most enterprise sales repeat the same pitch to each level until the decision maker. If the prospects really differ, you need more than one script. [M1 15:29 · YT 0:15:30]
- **A script is the definition of good.** Without it, reps aim at a target they cannot see. [M1 16:01 · YT 0:16:03]
- **Why it scales:** a frame plus a script means fewer qualifications to hire, faster onboarding, more predictable results. His analogy: drop the ball 6 inches from the hole and have reps practice that one putt, instead of teaching every golf shot. Do not make reps also be the therapist, product expert, coach and consultant. [M1 16:54 · YT 0:16:54] [M1 17:05 · YT 0:17:08]

## The frame: VSL (video sales letter)

- **What a frame is.** The information the prospect consumes before the conversation. The most scalable version today is a VSL. [M1 17:29 · YT 0:17:30] [M1 18:03 · YT 0:18:05]
- **Educate before the ask.** Under-educated prospects are why you need many calls. More information up front shortens the sales cycle and each call. He says this can sometimes double or triple sales efficiency. [M1 18:29 · YT 0:18:30]
- **VSL outline.** [M1 19:59 · YT 0:20:02] [M1 20:56 · YT 0:21:00]
  1. Promise: what this video will show.
  2. Pain: what it costs them to not fix it.
  3. Proof: varied case studies, so each viewer sees someone like them (demographics, beliefs, situation, outcome). "Most of this is a proof salad." [M1 19:34 · YT 0:19:37]
  4. Plan: what you will walk through.
  5. Picture: a visual roadmap if possible.
  6. Core persuasion: old way versus new way (your unique mechanism). [M1 21:22 · YT 0:21:24]
  7. FAQ: handle every objection. For each one: what they think, why it is wrong, what is right, proof. Treat it as a living document. Add new objections from sales calls. [M1 21:58 · YT 0:22:01] [M1 23:01 · YT 0:23:02]
  8. Call to action that makes the next call feel personal, with a simple question (for example "how many square feet is your kitchen?"). [M1 23:26 · YT 0:23:30]
  9. Proof roll: the rest of the video is happy customers. [M1 23:57 · YT 0:24:01]
- **The intro part is about 60 seconds.** [M1 20:56 · YT 0:21:00]
- **Length depends on friction.** Before an opt-in, keep it short, because every minute loses leads. After an opt-in or booking, make it as long as you want. [M1 21:40 · YT 0:21:40] [M1 28:02 · YT 0:28:03]
- **Simplest version:** before and after examples (compliant) for 10 minutes. [M1 24:54 · YT 0:24:55]
- **VSL sandwich.** Put a VSL at each step: on the page, after booking (sells the show), before the close. In home services, show a VSL on an iPad while you walk to the truck, then close. Do not email quotes. [M1 25:12 · YT 0:25:13] [M1 26:59 · YT 0:27:01]
- **If they did not watch it,** ask them to watch it now: "10 minutes now saves us 35 on the call." [M1 26:30 · YT 0:26:30]
- **The two levers of a sales motion are education and qualification.** BANT (budget, authority, need, timing) is the friction that gets the right people on the phone. [M1 27:40 · YT 0:27:41] [M1 27:51 · YT 0:27:54]
- **Scale note.** He runs about 50 active VSLs and films 2 to 3 new or updated ones each week. Start with one to three. Make separate paths for different avatars. [M1 28:14 · YT 0:28:17]

## The script: intro, disco, pitch, close

He teaches two outlines (CLOSER, and intro-disco-pitch-close). Pick one. This training uses intro, disco, pitch, close. [M1 12:55 · YT 0:12:55] [M1 28:51 · YT 0:28:55]

- **Shorter scripts sell more.** In about 90% of scripts he reviews, his team cuts 50 to 75% and sales go up. Reps learn the wrong lessons from random wins ("sneezing before the offer"). Goal: word concision. A 20 to 30 minute call instead of 45 doubles team efficiency. [M1 30:00 · YT 0:30:01] [M1 30:49 · YT 0:30:50] [M1 30:59 · YT 0:31:00]
- **Intro.** In the US: name, company, recorded line, reason for the call. Then the agenda and a small yes ("Sound good?"). Keep it tight. [M1 29:00 · YT 0:29:02] [M1 31:12 · YT 0:31:15]
- **Disco (discovery).** The most important part. "If you nail the disco, the pitch and the close are easy." [M1 32:00 · YT 0:32:02]
  - Current state versus desired state. You need a gap. [M1 29:34 · YT 0:29:38] [M1 32:29 · YT 0:32:30]
  - Pain cycle ("pulling teeth"): obstacle, why, then specifics. Rotate "Tell me more about that", "Can you give me an example?", "Say more about that." Two of these are commands, which keeps the frame. [M1 33:05 · YT 0:33:05]
  - No urgency means you did not dig deep enough into pain. [M1 32:33 · YT 0:32:35] [M1 33:10 · YT 0:33:10]
  - Recap, label, confirm: "So it sounds like the real issue is X. Does that sound right?" [M1 34:44 · YT 0:34:45]
- **Pitch.** Never longer than about two minutes. Rule of threes: problem, solution, benefit, three times, each with a confirm. Edify the person who will deliver (the closer is not the expert). [M1 29:52 · YT 0:29:50] [M1 35:48 · YT 0:35:50] [M1 36:00 · YT 0:36:00]
- **Ask.** If they know the price range, "Ready to get started?" If not, "It is expensive. It's $X. Does that work for you?" Then stop talking. He says reps who wait at least eight seconds close 30% more. [M1 37:50 · YT 0:37:55] [M1 38:49 · YT 0:38:51]

## Tonality

- Five controls: loud enough, slow enough, clear enough (always); pause to emphasize and raise pitch to ask for a response (conditional). [M1 38:58 · YT 0:39:03]
- The ask gets both: raise pitch, then pause. Scripts mark short, medium and long pauses. [M1 40:01 · YT 0:40:01]
- Later he gives 150 to 170 words per minute as the pace most people can follow, and says pausing 8+ seconds after the ask gives 23 to 40% more closes. [M4 53:12 · YT 3:14:41] [M4 53:32 · YT 3:15:01]

## Analogies

- Analogies move the most information per word, so they shorten the sale. Weave one analogy through a VSL. [M1 40:24 · YT 0:40:25] [M1 41:01 · YT 0:41:02]
- His examples by point: [M1 42:27 · YT 0:42:29] [M1 43:02 · YT 0:43:03] [M1 44:00 · YT 0:44:00]
  - You need all of it: the car needs wheels, engine and gas; three legs of a stool.
  - One constraint limits everything: weakest link; four lanes into one lane.
  - It takes time: braces take years; you cannot tan in one 20-hour session.
  - Do not buy cheapest: the cheapest surgeon, tattoo artist or parachute.
  - Stop waiting: the water will not get warmer.
  - Will it work for me: gravity does not care who jumps; follow the recipe, get the same cake.
