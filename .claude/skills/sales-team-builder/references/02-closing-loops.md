# 02. Closing loops, follow-up meetings and referrals

Source: Section 2a (the script part), about 0:45:00 to 1:15:00. The owner uses this file to decide what to teach the team about objections. Teach the 80/20 version first.

## The job is "no"

- If everyone says yes, you only need a checkout page. Sales is for the people who say anything but yes. A **loop** is what you say when they do not say yes. Then you ask again. [M1 45:13 · YT 0:45:17] [M1 45:43 · YT 0:45:40]
- Order of the loops: triage with the first loop, then five universal closes, with AAA between each, then five specific objections. [M1 45:55 · YT 0:45:59]

## First loop: confirm value, then triage

- "Completely hear you. Do you mind if I ask a quick question? Do you think this could help you get closer to your goal?" It is directional (closer), not absolute, so yes is easy. [M1 46:05 · YT 0:46:06]
- If yes, you can ask for the sale again. He says the first loop alone closes probably 25% of sales. [M1 46:55 · YT 0:46:56]
- If not, triage: "What's your main concern? What are you afraid of having happen?" The answer is usually decision maker, time, timing, fit (preferences) or money (value). [M1 46:59 · YT 0:47:00]

## Five universal closes (work on any objection)

He believes most objections are not real. People need to be asked again and reassured. Across his teams, the reps who ask the most times close the most. The closes let you ask again without annoying people. [M1 47:31 · YT 0:47:30] [M1 48:10 · YT 0:48:12]

1. **Reason close.** The reason they gave is the reason to do it. "The fact that timing is hard is the reason to start now." [M1 48:22 · YT 0:48:20]
2. **Hypothetical close.** Isolate the one variable: "If this was solved, would you do it?" [M1 49:16 · YT 0:49:21]
3. **Zoom out (pull the parachute).** "You're trying to lose weight, we sell weight loss. What part am I missing?" Get out of the dog fight. [M1 49:57 · YT 0:49:59]
4. **One to 10.** "What would make this a 10?" Or "Why not a one?" Or: nobody is a 10 before they start. [M1 51:08 · YT 0:51:09]
5. **Best case, worst case.** Best case it changes your life. Worst case (with a guarantee) there is little risk. The third door, doing nothing, guarantees no result. Doing nothing is the riskier choice. [M1 52:10 · YT 0:52:11] [M1 53:00 · YT 0:53:01]

## AAA between every close

- Acknowledge, associate, ask. Agree with them, associate what they said with your best customers ("that's exactly what our best clients ask"), then ask the next question. It lets you "stay in the pocket" and ask again. [M1 53:31 · YT 0:53:32] [M1 53:58 · YT 0:54:00]
- Use questions, not statements, during objections. People can argue with statements. They answer questions. [M1 55:26 · YT 0:55:26]
- Use a foil: "Can I share what I told a client last week who had the same concern?" [M1 55:58 · YT 0:56:02]

## Five specific objections (the deeper 20%)

The five are time, money, decision maker, preferences (fit) and stall. Money is most often the real one, behind the others. [M1 56:29 · YT 0:56:30]

- **Time.** Core argument: it is about priorities, not time. When-then fallacy ("I'll go to the hospital once I'm healthy"). Learn it while busy so it lasts. Trade time from their least important activity. [M1 57:14 · YT 0:57:17] [M1 57:59 · YT 0:58:00]
- **Money (has money).** Core: it is a value problem. Frames: it is good that it is a lot; if it were a tenth of the price, would you believe it; "if all this does is X, is it worth it?"; "if it were free, would you take it?"; "could you do it for less?" means they decided; good, fast, cheap, pick two; the money gets spent either way. [M1 58:59 · YT 0:59:02] [M1 59:58 · YT 1:00:01] [M1 1:01:01 · YT 1:01:01]
- **Money (truly does not have it).** It is resourcefulness, not resources. Help them find the first payment. [M1 1:02:06 · YT 1:02:08]
- **Decision maker.** Best fix: get every decision maker on the call. If you cannot, four steps: what if they say no; what specifically would they object to; do they approve of your current situation; ask for support, not permission. [M1 1:02:38 · YT 1:02:40] [M1 1:02:58 · YT 1:03:00] [M1 1:04:23 · YT 1:04:25]
- **Preferences.** Pain of change: you change when staying the same hurts more. Make the trade explicit. [M1 1:04:31 · YT 1:04:36] [M1 1:05:02 · YT 1:05:03]
- **Stall.** You are talking to the person in power. Show that inaction has a cost. Frames: burn you twice; three decisions (product, company, me); direction, not perfection; information, not time; extrapolate the pain five years out. [M1 1:05:27 · YT 1:05:30] [M1 1:06:06 · YT 1:06:08] [M1 1:07:15 · YT 1:07:18] [M1 1:07:57 · YT 1:07:58] [M1 1:08:38 · YT 1:08:40]

## How much to teach

- He estimates (says he has not pulled the exact data) that about 30 to 40% close on the first ask, and the universal loops bring the total to about 80%. The last 20% are edge cases that make a rep top 1%. Teach the team the 80/20 version first. [M1 1:09:42 · YT 1:09:40] [M1 1:09:59 · YT 1:10:03]

## If they do not buy: book a meeting from a meeting

- Never hang up without the next step. Save 2 to 3 minutes at the end of each call to book it. [M1 1:10:38 · YT 1:10:40] [M1 1:11:01 · YT 1:11:01]
- Three reasons to book now: it saves time, neither of you adds a to-do, you can always move it. If no, suggest two times; then shrink the commitment (15 minutes); then ask "when were you planning to let me know?" and book that time. "If you can't close the appointment, you're not going to close the sale." [M1 1:12:56 · YT 1:12:59] [M1 1:13:29 · YT 1:13:30] [M1 1:13:59 · YT 1:14:01]

## If they buy: ask for referrals on the call

- "Who else do you know who's as awesome as you are?" Then a three-way intro. He says referrals are responsible for 20 to 30% of sales, and about half of referrals come from asking on the call. [M1 1:11:30 · YT 1:11:32] [M1 1:11:59 · YT 1:12:02]
