# 04. The recruiting machine: build it, then feed it

Source: Section 2c "How to Build the Machine" [M2 21:11 · YT 1:36:17] and 2d "How to Feed the Machine" [M2 33:20 · YT 1:48:26].

## Treat hiring like customer acquisition

- Talent acquisition is a funnel, like customer acquisition. Build the funnel first, then feed it. He says the steps were added or removed based on problems they hit. [M2 21:24 · YT 1:36:29] [M2 21:54 · YT 1:37:00]

## The steps

1. **Traffic** (see "Feed the machine" below). [M2 21:54 · YT 1:37:00]
2. **Application page.** Do not let HR write it. It is a sales letter. Sections he shows: sales team vision, who this is for, what we offer, what we expect, results, requirements, location, pay and commission, benefits, core values. Put a VSL above the application that sells the company and vision. Tell the truth about the hours. [M2 22:14 · YT 1:37:20] [M2 23:25 · YT 1:38:30]
3. **60-second selfie video,** required. They never hired anyone who skipped it. [M2 22:46 · YT 1:37:54] [M2 22:53 · YT 1:38:02]
4. **Group call first,** not one-on-ones. This fixed his outbound hiring bottleneck: HR took 20 calls a week, passed 4, the manager offered 1. After the switch, they hit a goal they had missed for three quarters within six weeks. [M2 23:17 · YT 1:38:25] [M2 23:53 · YT 1:39:01]
   - Set hard expectations. His green flag: one or two people leave the call. His team works 6 days a week, 12 hours a day; set your own real expectations. [M2 24:44 · YT 1:39:50] [M2 24:54 · YT 1:40:02]
   - Show "what it looks like versus what it feels like": learning is fast early, earnings come later at the edge of mastery. Expect boredom from repetition. [M2 25:55 · YT 1:41:00] [M2 26:53 · YT 1:42:00]
   - Q&A: 60-second background and "why here?". Interrupt to test how quick they are. Tell them yes or no by text afterwards. [M2 27:53 · YT 1:43:02] [M2 28:54 · YT 1:44:00]
5. **Technical (skill) call.** Treat it as their first training session. Send the script before. Role play and interrupt them 30 to 40 times with feedback. Unprepared or late: end the call. Bonus signal: they ask closing questions like "What would it take for me to be on the team?" [M2 29:19 · YT 1:44:26] [M2 29:53 · YT 1:45:00]
6. **Culture call.** Quality control for a manager who is desperate to fill a seat. The culture interviewer unsells the job until the candidate sells themselves. Ask: why us, why sales, 5 to 10 year goals, 2 year goals, what will you do well, where might you fail. Give one candid piece of feedback and watch the reaction. [M2 30:57 · YT 1:46:03] [M2 31:24 · YT 1:46:30] [M2 31:54 · YT 1:47:01]
7. **Job offer.** Offer, start date, handbook and pre-work, VSLs on the vision. See who does the homework before day one. [M2 32:41 · YT 1:47:49] [M2 32:54 · YT 1:48:00]

## Feed the machine: how much to spend

- **Story.** An insurance owner paid $500 per referral for a broker worth about $250,000 a year in gross profit. He raised it to $25,000. He says the company went from $10 million to $400 million. [M2 33:33 · YT 1:48:40] [M2 34:03 · YT 1:49:11] [M2 35:10 · YT 1:50:17]
- **The math.** Lifetime gross profit per employee (cap at one year) x the percent who make it through, divided by 3 = the most you should pay to acquire one hire. His example: $250,000 x 50% = $125,000; / 3 = about $40,000. Aim for 3 to 1 in year one, factor in churn. [M2 34:41 · YT 1:49:52] [M2 34:53 · YT 1:50:00]
- **Always be hiring,** so no rep thinks the company depends on them. [M2 35:22 · YT 1:50:30]

## Sources, in his order of preference

1. **Employee referrals.** Pay the bonus at the activation point (for example day 90), not at hire. The referrer becomes an internal champion. [M2 35:57 · YT 1:51:02]
2. **Posting content** related to the role, and emailing your customer list. [M2 36:46 · YT 1:51:50]
3. **Recruiters on contingency only,** never retainer. Negotiate out exclusivity by asking for more hires than one firm can deliver. Run their candidates through your own group call. He says this took one portfolio sales team from 0 to 50 activated reps in 90 days (80 hired). He estimates $5,000 to $10,000 per salesperson is about industry average. [M2 36:54 · YT 1:52:02] [M2 37:22 · YT 1:52:31] [M2 37:52 · YT 1:53:01]
4. **Ads and job boards.** You get a lot of trash unless you are good at marketing. [M2 38:15 · YT 1:53:20]
5. **Forums, communities and direct outreach** ("Do you know anyone who is a clone of you?"). [M2 38:34 · YT 1:53:40] [M2 38:54 · YT 1:54:02]
- A repeated role needs its own machine. A one-off role is a better fit for a recruiter. [M2 39:05 · YT 1:54:10]
