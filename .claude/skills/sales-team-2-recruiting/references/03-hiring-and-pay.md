# 03. Who to hire, in what order, and how to pay them

Source: Section 2b "Who to Look For" [M1 1:14:59 · YT 1:15:01] to [M2 21:11 · YT 1:36:17].

## Prove it before you get help

- **Never two news.** Do not put a new person on a new process. If it fails, you cannot tell if the person or the process is the problem. Prove the process yourself first. [M2 0:25 · YT 1:15:33]
- **If cash flow allows, hire more than one at once.** Training one rep takes as long as training five. With five, you can see who is good. If all five fail, the process is the problem. [M2 0:46 · YT 1:15:50] [M2 0:55 · YT 1:16:03]

## Who to look for

- **Three Ps plus one.** Has the person sold this prospect, this process, this product? You want two of the three. If you can only weigh one, prefer the prospect. Plus one: at least one year of experience. [M2 1:23 · YT 1:16:31] [M2 2:55 · YT 1:18:02]
  - Process includes high or low volume, and hot or cold traffic (word of mouth and organic are warmer than ads, which are warmer than cold calls). [M2 2:25 · YT 1:17:30]
- **Three traits.** [M2 3:20 · YT 1:18:27]
  - Intelligence = rate of learning = how fast they use feedback. Test it with live coaching in the interview. [M2 3:24 · YT 1:18:31]
  - Work ethic = how prepared they are, how much they practiced the script, the quality of their questions, how fast they follow up. "You are the lead." [M2 3:47 · YT 1:18:54]
  - Humility = ego does not block learning. A rep who wants to change the script before selling is a bad sign. [M2 4:15 · YT 1:19:25]
- **Hire for the lowest total cost to reach your standard.** His newer view: hire the person who needs the fewest resources to meet the standard. His formula: cost to get them up to speed = gap / (intelligence x training). A smart hire keeps improving after they reach the standard. [M2 4:57 · YT 1:20:06] [M2 5:40 · YT 1:20:50] [M2 5:54 · YT 1:21:00]

## Order of hiring

- **Little inflow (you must create demand):** hire setters first. **Lots of inflow (audience, organic, word of mouth) and you close:** hire closers first. [M2 6:55 · YT 1:22:01]
- **His own model:** he only recruits setters and promotes 100% of closers from inside ("farm system"). Benefits he lists: faster closer ramp, closers stay longer, fewer botched sales. He says they have not fired a closer in over 12 months. SDR churn happens mostly in the first 60 days. [M2 7:23 · YT 1:22:32] [M2 7:57 · YT 1:23:02]
- **SDR = setter. BC (business consultant) = closer.** [M2 7:49 · YT 1:22:55]
- **With two or more remote reps, spread time zones** to cover more hours. [M2 8:42 · YT 1:23:49]
- **Hiring a leader first** is the fastest path but needs the most skill to judge. It is easy to be fooled by good salespeople. Use with caution. [M2 8:53 · YT 1:24:01]

## Hiring a sales manager

- Do not promote your best closer by default. You can lose your top producer and not improve the team. [M2 9:42 · YT 1:24:51] [M2 9:55 · YT 1:25:03]
- Look for: even temper, top 25% performer, cares about the team. Watch who teammates already ask for help, and who stays late to help. [M2 10:22 · YT 1:25:30]
- Red flags: will not take calls, wants to change everything on day one. [M2 10:54 · YT 1:26:03]
- Killer question: "What numbers do you track?" then "How does what you do change those numbers?" You want behaviors for each metric. [M2 11:24 · YT 1:26:30] [M2 11:34 · YT 1:26:42]
- Traits: ownership, high standards, low ego, drive, invests in people. Tools they should name: modeling, role play, incentives. [M2 11:54 · YT 1:27:01]
- More questions: what do you observe and how do you correct it; biggest accomplishment; biggest problem solved on a sales team (be specific); biggest loss and why; favorite part of the job. [M2 12:34 · YT 1:27:40] [M2 12:53 · YT 1:28:01]

## Pay

- **It is cheaper to pay better.** His setters earn what closers earn at most companies. Cost per rep goes up, but customer acquisition cost goes down, ramp time goes down, conversion goes up, churn goes down, and you get more and better applicants. Keep "pressure from behind" so the team never has leverage on you. [M2 13:32 · YT 1:28:39] [M2 13:54 · YT 1:29:01]
- **Pay setters and closers on closes,** not on sets or shows. It aligns everyone on the same goal. Exception: a long enterprise cycle may pay on qualified shows. [M2 14:48 · YT 1:29:55] [M2 14:52 · YT 1:30:02] [M2 20:57 · YT 1:36:02]
- **OTE split 50/50.** Set on-target earnings (OTE) from the skill the seat needs. Then 50% base, 50% variable. He credits a Vista Private Equity study for inbound closers. Base covers needs, so reps do not get "commission breath". [M2 15:56 · YT 1:31:01] [M2 16:34 · YT 1:31:44] [M2 16:54 · YT 1:32:02]
- **Flat per-sale rate, from the math.** His example: $100,000 OTE, $50,000 variable, about 50 working weeks = $1,000 per week. Expected volume: 9 calls a day, 6 show, 2 close, 6 days a week = 12 closes a week. $1,000 / 12 = about $83 per sale. Recalculate with your own OTE and lead flow. [M2 17:21 · YT 1:32:30] [M2 17:56 · YT 1:33:02] [M2 18:23 · YT 1:33:31]
- **Accelerators later.** Tiered per-sale rates that rise through the month (his example tiers: first 30 at $75, then $95, $105, $125, more). The blend still averages the same rate. Only add this once you have good metrics across the team. [M2 18:45 · YT 1:33:54] [M2 18:55 · YT 1:34:02] [M2 19:53 · YT 1:35:00]
- **If you must pay a percentage,** use cash collected up front (or up-front gross margin), not contract value. He prefers a flat rate per sale. [M2 20:24 · YT 1:35:30] [M2 20:53 · YT 1:35:59]
- Self-generated, door-to-door or field teams are different: back into your allowable acquisition cost and usually leave pay uncapped. [M2 15:56 · YT 1:31:01]
